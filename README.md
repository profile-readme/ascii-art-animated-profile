# ascii-art-animated-profile

English | [日本語](README.ja.md) | [简体中文](README.zh-CN.md) | [繁體中文](README.zh-TW.md)

Tiny animated kaomoji and ASCII art for your GitHub profile README. A cat that blinks, a
table that gets flipped and put back, the 2channel classic Owata jumping in despair, a
greentext that types itself. One Markdown line, no sign-up, no JavaScript.

## Quick start

Paste one of these into your profile `README.md`, the repository named after your
username:

```markdown
![Owata](https://readmes.pages.dev/ascii/owata)
![A random cat](https://readmes.pages.dev/ascii/cat)
![Today's 2channel classic](https://readmes.pages.dev/ascii/2ch/daily)
```

The first line always shows Owata. The second shows a random cat on every page view.
The third picks one 2channel classic per day, so your profile changes daily without you
touching it.

Use HTML when you want to center things or put several side by side:

```html
<p align="center">
  <img src="https://readmes.pages.dev/ascii/kitsune/card" alt="kitsune">
  <img src="https://readmes.pages.dev/ascii/suya/card" alt="sleepy face">
</p>
```

### About the tags

The kawaii tag collects the soft, cheerful faces: bears, foxes, waving hands, sleepy
cheeks. These suit a profile that wants to feel friendly and a little playful.

The cat tag is for cat people. It mixes the Western ASCII cat face with Japanese cat
kaomoji, plus Giko, the famously grumpy 2channel cat.

The 2ch tag holds Japanese textboard classics. 2channel, now known as 5channel, grew an
entire culture of ASCII art called AA. Characters such as Mona and Giko became mascots,
and short faces like Owata, Kita and orz turned into everyday slang that is still used
across Japanese social media.

The reddit tag gathers faces that became staples of Reddit comment threads, such as the
shrug, the look of disapproval and the Lenny face. Several of them started elsewhere and
found their widest audience there.

The 4chan tag covers the greentext storytelling format and the smug face. Only harmless,
work-safe pieces are included.

The yami tag comes from yami kawaii, a Japanese style that mixes cute with gloomy. Here it
means gentle sadness only: rain, tears, the pleading pien face and sleepiness. Nothing in
the collection refers to self-harm.

The meme tag overlaps with several others and collects the pieces most people will
recognize at a glance.

## A short history of text faces

People have drawn faces with keyboard characters for as long as there have been message
boards. Western emoticons such as `:-)` are read sideways, with the head tilted to the left.
Japanese kaomoji grew up separately and are read straight on. That small difference shaped
two quite different traditions.

Because kaomoji face the reader, the eyes and mouth carry the emotion. `(^_^)` is calm and
happy, `(;_;)` is crying, and `(ﾟДﾟ)` is shocked. Japanese computers also offered full-width
characters, katakana and many symbols from the start. Writers combined them freely into
faces with arms, sweat drops, sparkles and props.

On Japanese textboards in the late 1990s and 2000s, especially 2channel, this grew into a
full art form known as AA, short for ASCII art. Regulars drew characters such as Mona and
Giko over many lines. They passed them around, edited them and gave them catchphrases.
Short faces from those boards turned into everyday words. Owata means "it's over" and Kita
means "it's here". The kneeling figure orz stands for total defeat.

Many of those faces later traveled to English-speaking sites. The table flip arrived from
Japanese forums and became a standard reaction to frustration. The shrug borrows the
katakana character ツ as a face. The look of disapproval uses a letter from the Kannada
script for its narrowed eyes. The Lenny face came together on 4chan from scattered Unicode
pieces and then spread across Reddit.

Greentext belongs to the same family. On 4chan, a line that starts with `>` turns green,
and people began telling short stories one line at a time, opening with `>be me`. The
format is now recognized far beyond the site where it began.

This collection keeps pieces short, one to three lines, so they fit neatly into a README.
It adds small movements that the originals only implied: a blink, a wave, a hop or a typed
line.

## Ideas for your profile

A single small piece near your name works like a signature. Something quiet such as the
sleepy face or the blinking cat sets a friendly tone without pulling attention from your
projects.

A header row of two or three pieces side by side gives a profile some personality.
Combine one animated piece with one static piece so the page does not feel busy.

Daily mode suits people who like their profile to feel alive. Pick a tag that matches
your taste, add `daily`, and visitors on different days will see something new.

Pieces can also mark sections in a long README. Put a small piece above "Projects" and
another above "Contact" to break up the page.

If your profile has a theme, match it. A dark profile with the rainbow option feels
playful, while a single color that matches your accent color looks tidy and deliberate.

## Options

Options are path segments after `/ascii/`, written in any order. A trailing `.svg` is
allowed and case does not matter. If you mistype something, the image becomes a small
pink notice naming the position of the unknown option.

### What to show

| Option | Effect |
|---|---|
| a name from the table | That art set |
| `kawaii`, `meme`, `cat`, `yami`, `reddit`, `4chan`, `2ch` | A random set from that tag |
| `random` | A random set from everything (the default) |
| `daily` | One set per day, combined with a tag if you like |

### Look

| Option | Effect |
|---|---|
| `size-N`, `size-Nrem` | Font size in pixels, 12 to 64 (default 22) |
| `px` | Pin exact pixels instead of rem (see below) |
| `card` | A rounded card behind the art |
| `border-N` | Card border in pixels, 0 to 8 |
| `color-RRGGBB` | Text color in hex, without `#` |
| `rainbow` | Text slowly cycles through soft colors |
| `auto`, `light`, `dark`, `transparent` | Theme (default `auto`) |
| `warm` | Warm readmes palette instead of GitHub's own text colors (default) |
| `left`, `center` | Alignment override |

### Motion

| Option | Effect |
|---|---|
| `speed-N` | Playback speed in percent, 25 to 400 |
| `bounce` | The whole art gently bobs up and down |
| `static` | First frame only, no animation |

In auto mode the text is dark on light backgrounds and light on dark ones, matching each
viewer's GitHub theme. The greentext keeps its traditional green unless you pick a color.

### Sizing in rem

Every image is sized in rem, where 1rem is 16 pixels. With default browser settings it looks
exactly like a pixel size. When a reader has raised the default font size in their browser,
often to make text easier to read, the art grows with it, just like the text around it. You
can also write sizes in rem, as in `size-2rem`. Add `px` when a piece must keep an exact
pixel size, for example to line up with other images in a row.

## How it works

Each art set is stored as a short list of text frames with a duration for each frame. When
you load a URL, a small Cloudflare Pages Function turns that list into an SVG. Every frame
becomes a group of text lines, and a few lines of CSS switch the groups on and off in
turn.

Because the animation is pure CSS inside the image, it plays anywhere an image can be
shown. GitHub's image proxy passes it through untouched, and no script ever runs on your
profile.

The text stays real text inside the SVG rather than being turned into pixels. That keeps
the files tiny, often well under two kilobytes, and lets the colors follow light and dark
mode.

### Why SVG instead of a GIF

Animated GIFs are the usual way to put motion in a README, but they suit text art poorly.
A GIF stores every frame as pixels, so even a tiny face becomes tens of kilobytes. It also
cannot adapt to dark mode, because its colors are fixed. On high-resolution screens the
letters turn blurry or blocky.

An SVG of text avoids all three problems. The file holds only the characters and a few
lines of timing, so it stays small no matter how large you draw it. The browser renders
the letters with a real font at full sharpness. The colors can follow the viewer's theme,
and size, speed and color are just parts of the URL rather than separate files to export.

There is one tradeoff. A GIF looks the same everywhere, while text in an SVG depends on
the fonts available on each device. For short kaomoji this rarely matters, and it is the
reason the collection avoids large multi-line drawings that need exact alignment.

### Fonts and alignment

The art is drawn with the viewer's own monospace font. Most systems have one that covers
Japanese characters, so the faces look right nearly everywhere. Spacing can still differ
a little between fonts, especially for full-width Japanese symbols in multi-line pieces
such as Mona. Pieces that move sideways, like the walking cat and the greentext, are
built from plain ASCII so they line up exactly.

The canvas is sized with a little spare room on purpose, so the art never gets clipped
when a viewer's font runs wider than expected.

### Caching

A URL with a fixed name is cached for a day, which makes it load instantly. Random URLs
are sent with caching turned off, so GitHub fetches a new pick on most views. Daily URLs
are cached until midnight UTC and then change.

## Accessibility

Each image has a title such as "ASCII art: Owata (animated)", and you should add alt text
when you embed it. The examples above show how. Because the art is real text, screen
readers may also read the characters. That is usually harmless but can sound odd, which
is another reason to give a clear alt text.

Animations stop for anyone who has turned on "reduce motion" in their system settings.
They see the first frame as a still image instead. The `static` option does the same for
everyone.

The default colors meet WCAG AA contrast against GitHub's light and dark backgrounds. If
you pick your own color, check that it stays readable in both themes.

## Add your own art

The demo never accepts uploads or custom text, by design. To add your own sets, run your
own copy. It is free on Cloudflare Pages and takes a few minutes.

You need Node.js 20 or newer, `make`, and a free Cloudflare account. There is no
`npm install`, because the project has no dependencies.

1. Click "Use this template" on GitHub, create your repository, and clone it.
2. Put your own name and repository link in `site.config.json`.
3. Add entries to `src/ascii/arts.js`, following the existing ones.
4. Run `make test`, then deploy.

```sh
npx wrangler pages project create my-badges --production-branch production
make deploy PROJECT=my-badges
```

Your art is then served at `https://my-badges.pages.dev/ascii/<name>`.

### Writing a set

A set needs a name for the URL, a title, one or more tags, and a list of frames. Each
frame is a list of one to three lines. Give either one duration for every frame in
milliseconds, or a list with a duration per frame.

```js
{ name: "my-cat", title: "My cat", tags: ["cat"], d: [1500, 200],
  frames: [["(=^-^=)"], ["(=-.-=)"]] },
```

Short pauses make blinks, and longer holds give the eye time to rest. For anything that
moves sideways, set `align: "left"` and use plain ASCII with spaces, so every font keeps
the steps even. Two helpers at the top of the file build typing and walking animations
for you.

The tests check that names are unique and frames have one to three lines. They also check
that every set renders without errors and that no frame contains an emoji, since emoji
fonts would break the monospace look.

## FAQ

### Why does the art look slightly different on my phone?

Each device draws the text with its own font, so widths and shapes vary a little. The
pieces are chosen to survive that, but a full-width symbol may sit a bit differently.

### Can I write my own text in the URL?

No. Accepting free text would let anyone put arbitrary words on an image served from this
site, so the demo only knows its own collection. Your own deployment can contain whatever
you add to the art list.

### The random art never changes on my profile

Your browser may be holding on to its copy. Try a hard refresh, or use `daily` for a
predictable daily change that does not depend on caching.

### Can I use these outside GitHub?

Yes. Anything that can show an image URL works, including personal sites, other code hosts
and forum signatures.

### Can I suggest new art for the demo?

Suggestions are welcome as issues on the repository. Good candidates are short, widely
known, work-safe, and readable in a plain monospace font. Pieces built around a commercial
character or brand are left out on purpose, to keep the collection free of trademark
questions.

### Is it okay to use these kaomoji?

They are folk art that people have shared freely for decades. See the license section for
how this project treats them.

## Troubleshooting

### The image shows a pink "unknown option" notice

One of the path segments is not a known name or option. The notice tells you its
position, counting from the first segment after `/ascii/`. Check the spelling against the
gallery and the option tables; names use hyphens, as in `table-flip`.

### The art is cut off at the edge

This is rare, because every image is sized with spare room. It can still happen with an
unusually wide fallback font. Making the art a little larger with `size-N` keeps the
proportions but gives more room, and the `card` option adds extra padding as well.

### The animation does not move

Check whether your system has "reduce motion" turned on. If so, the first frame is shown
on purpose. Some Markdown previewers in editors also show only the first frame of an SVG.
The real profile page on GitHub plays the animation.

### The colors look wrong in dark mode

If you set a fixed color with `color-RRGGBB`, it stays the same in both themes. Remove it
to let the text follow the viewer's theme, or choose a middle tone that reads well on both
light and dark backgrounds.

## Privacy

The demo stores nothing. There are no uploads, forms, cookies or analytics. Each image is
computed only from the URL path, and only a fixed list of options is accepted.

On GitHub, images are fetched through GitHub's proxy, so the demo normally never sees the
IP addresses of people viewing your profile. As with any website, Cloudflare may keep
standard request logs under its own [privacy policy](https://www.cloudflare.com/privacypolicy/).
